The problem. Cancer research data lives in intimidating tables. OncoMap asks: can a student actually see a cancer — where it sits in the body, which genes drive it, and how a classifier reads its signature?

The build. An interactive anatomical map and gene mapper in React/Vite, paired with a Python Streamlit classifier trained on TCGA cohorts. Pick a cancer type, explore its gene story, and watch the model classify genomic sub-types.

The outcome. A working educational research prototype covering six TCGA cohorts — public on GitHub, built end-to-end from data pipeline to interface.

MiniOS

Operating Systems · C · A Whole Tiny Computer, Built From Scratch

Most students study operating systems. MiniOS rebuilds one — a boot-to-shell miniature OS playground in C where every invisible decision a real OS makes becomes something you can watch, pause, rewind and break. Scheduler, memory, files, processes: no black boxes left.

  • BOOTBoot sequence & kernel log — watch it wake up, line by line, like a real machine.
  • CPUScheduler arena — Round Robin vs SJF vs Priority, raced live on the same processes (try the demo below).
  • RAMMemory map & paging — frames fill, pages fault, and fragmentation shows itself in colour.
  • DISKA toy file system + shell — create, write and read files through a working command line.
  • SYNCDeadlock theatre — trigger a deadlock on purpose, then watch the OS detect and break it.
